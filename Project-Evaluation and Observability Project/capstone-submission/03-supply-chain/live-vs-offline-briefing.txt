==============================================================================
 ⚠️  FINDING: A REQUIRED DATE FIELD MANUFACTURES DATES
==============================================================================

The live briefing carries THREE claims dated 2024-01-01:

    > A company identified only as 'Meridian' has drawn down emergency credit
      lines amid deteriorating cash flow. (industry_news, 2024-01-01)
    > Meridian has missed payments to subcontractors. (industry_news, 2024-01-01)
    > Meridian has retained restructuring advisers amid deteriorating cash flow.
      (industry_news, 2024-01-01)

All three come from data/meridian/news/ambiguous_meridian.txt. That article
CONTAINS NO DATE AT ALL:

    $ grep -inE 'date|20[0-9]{2}' data/meridian/news/ambiguous_meridian.txt
    (no matches — the article says 'this quarter' and nothing more)

The recorded fixture for the same article dates those claims 2026-03-09.
Two runs, two different invented dates, for an article that has none:

    recorded:  (industry_news, 2026-03-09)
    live:      (industry_news, 2024-01-01)

WHY THE MODEL HAS NO CHOICE. source_date is required and non-nullable in both
layers of the contract:

    supply_chain_risk/models.py
        class Claim(BaseModel):
            source_date: date          # not 'date | None'

    supply_chain_risk/news_extraction.py — tool schema
        "required": ["claim", "evidence", "source_date", "confidence", ...]

    the system prompt
        "Each claim carries the article's publication date as source_date"
        (no instruction for what to do when the article has no date)

The schema demands a date, the prompt assumes one exists, and the model cannot
return null — so it invents one. 2024-01-01 is a placeholder wearing the type of
evidence.

==============================================================================
 WHY THIS IS THE MOST INSTRUCTIVE CROSS-SYSTEM FINDING IN THE PACK
==============================================================================

System 2 solved exactly this problem and System 3 reintroduces it.

  System 2  Income.bonus_monthly is 'float | None'. The document is silent, the
            model returns null, and the pipeline records an honest absence.
            (see 02-mortgage-extraction/missing-field-run.txt)

  System 3  Claim.source_date is 'date'. The article is silent, null is not
            expressible, and the model fabricates 2024-01-01.

Same organisation, same principle, opposite outcome — decided entirely by whether
one field was declared nullable. A required field is not a guarantee that the data
exists; it is a guarantee that SOMETHING will be in that slot. When the underlying
fact is genuinely unavailable, a non-nullable field does not prevent a gap, it
CONVERTS THE GAP INTO A FABRICATION and strips the evidence that it was ever a gap.

The consequence is concrete. Section 3c of the brief asks how requiring a date
stops a time difference from reading as a contradiction. It does — but only for
claims whose dates are real. A fabricated 2024-01-01 would make genuinely current
financial-distress reporting look two years stale, and a reader triaging by
recency would deprioritise it. The guardrail is only as good as the field it
depends on, and here that field is silently synthetic.

FIX: declare source_date as 'date | None', instruct the prompt to return null when
the article carries no publication date, and render undated claims as
'(industry_news, date not stated)'. That is a one-line type change plus one prompt
sentence, and it converts a silent fabrication into a visible gap.
