--- reading this report ---
Brier score = mean squared error between stated confidence and the binary outcome.
Lower is better; 0.0 is perfect, 0.25 is the score of always saying 50%.

The umbrella/exclusions cell states 0.93 confidence and is wrong every single time
(acc=0.00), for a cell Brier of 0.865 — catastrophic. Yet the OVERALL Brier is only
0.291, which on its own reads as mediocre-but-tolerable rather than alarming.
Averaging over six samples dilutes two catastrophic ones with four good ones.

That is the whole argument for slicing: an aggregate answers 'how are we doing?',
a slice answers 'where are we failing?'. Only the second is actionable.
