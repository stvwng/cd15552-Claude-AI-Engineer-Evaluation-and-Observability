WHAT THIS REVEALS THAT THE UNPERTURBED RUN DOES NOT
---------------------------------------------------
On unperturbed input, on_time_delivery_rate is simply 'Contested' with 95 vs 78.
You cannot tell from that whether the system detects conflict by exact inequality,
by an absolute threshold, or by a relative one — nor how close two values may be
before the disagreement stops being reported. The sweep answers all three:

  * The test is RELATIVE, not absolute. An 8.6-point gap (95 vs 86.4) would be
    contested, while an 8.6-point gap at the low end (86.6 vs 78) is not, because
    the denominator is the larger value.
  * The tolerance is 10% of the LARGER value, so the band is asymmetric. Sources
    reporting 78 and 86.6 are called 'corroborated' despite an 8.6-point spread on
    a metric where a single point of on-time delivery is operationally meaningful.
  * Escalation is downstream of classification. The ESCALATE flag appears and
    disappears with the section, because _escalation() fires on
    (metric in HIGH_IMPACT and classification == CONTESTED). Nothing about the
    magnitude of the disagreement is consulted, only the boolean.

THE OPERATIONAL POINT
---------------------
A supplier reporting 86% against our measured 78% would be silently filed as
'Well-Established, corroborated across 2 sources' — the same badge the briefing
gives a metric where both sources genuinely agree. The 8-point gap is not shown,
not flagged, and not escalated. A reader has no way to distinguish 'both sources
said 78' from 'sources said 78 and 86.6, which we decided was close enough'.

A single relative tolerance is doing double duty here: absorbing measurement noise
AND deciding what counts as a business-relevant disagreement. Those are different
questions, and 10% is a defensible answer to the first but a poor one to the second
for a service-level metric. The fix is per-metric tolerances (on-time delivery might
warrant 2%, defect-rate ppm might warrant 20%), plus rendering the spread even when
the metric is classified as corroborated, so 'close enough' remains visible.
